Abstract:

We develop from scratch a complete equity options framework for Pakistan — encompassing pricing, volatility surface construction, risk management, and margining — for a market where no traded options exist to calibrate against. This represents the first end-to-end derivatives infrastructure built for the KSE-100 index and Pakistani single stocks, solving simultaneously the two problems that had blocked exchange-traded options in Pakistan for nearly a decade: arbitrage-free pricing at inception and a robust margining framework for risky volatility strategies.

Using Dupire's break-even implied volatility framework, we construct arbitrage-free volatility surfaces for the KSE-100 index and individual equities by solving for the implied volatility that renders a delta-hedged option seller's cumulative gamma P&L exactly zero in expectation over historical price paths. This approach requires no market-quoted option prices and produces surfaces at inception with correct skew for index options and smile for single stock options, consistent with their respective return distributions.

For the KSE-100 index, we document a negative volatility skew with a 25-delta skew premium ranging from 9% at 7-day tenor to 11.5% at 28-day tenor, consistent with crash-o-phobia and the negative skewness of realized index returns. Arbitrage-free put and call price surfaces are derived across tenors of 7, 14, 21, and 28 business days with put-call parity verified throughout. Risk-neutral densities extracted via the Breeden-Litzenberger formula reveal asymmetric tail risk: the implied 1st percentile 28-day return is −12.7% versus a 99th percentile of +7%, directly quantifying the crash protection premium priced by the market. Longer-dated options should be introduced only after NCCPL accumulates sufficient implied volatility history to analyze and margin vega risk appropriately.

A margining framework for short volatility strategies is calibrated empirically: naked short straddles require a 2.7× margin multiplier over delta-hedged positions, and 2.2× for strangles. We characterize two market regimes — trending and choppy driftless — demonstrating that delta hedging reduces risk under drift but amplifies losses in choppy markets where realized variance systematically exceeds implied variance near ATM strikes. Additional margin is warranted when the implied return skew extracted from option prices underestimates the realized skew of underlying returns.

Finally, we derive a KSE-100 volatility index analogous to CBOE VIX via model-free variance swap replication. Accurate risk management and volatility index construction both require multiple listed strikes across tenors of 7, 14, 21, 28, and 35 business days — a contract specification recommendation embedded in this framework. The entire infrastructure is stress-tested through the February 2026 KSE-100 crash, validating the framework under live market conditions before a single option has traded.

Keywords: implied volatility surface, break-even volatility, Breeden-Litzenberger, risk-neutral density, volatility skew, frontier markets, options pricing at inception, variance risk premium, margining, volatility index, KSE-100
